# About Me

Hi, I'm Titus. I work in product data at SGS Engineering, where I help make sense of the information behind the products customers see and buy online.

My work sits somewhere between **data, ecommerce, products, and commercial operations**. Depending on the day, I might be analysing sales performance, building product bundles, managing pricing structures, improving product attributes, working on SEO, or merchandising products across the website.

I like being the person who can step back, understand how those pieces connect, and turn something complicated into something useful.

# How I Got Here

The path here wasn't exactly planned.

Growing up, sport was a big part of my life. I played a lot of rugby and eventually made it into both the **Harlequins** and **London Broncos** academies, with the aim of playing professionally.

A series of concussions, followed by COVID, eventually brought that chapter to an end.

It left me having to figure out what came next.

During a careers meeting, I discovered that you could actually study sport at university. That sounded like a pretty good idea at the time, so I ended up at **Nottingham Trent University**.

At the time, I didn't realise quite how useful it would prove to be.

University introduced me to research methods, statistical analysis, biomechanics, physiology, and evidence-based decision making. Somewhere along the way, I realised I particularly enjoyed the analytical side of things — taking a messy set of information, working through it, and eventually getting to an answer.

I remember one piece of coursework in particular. A group of us were in the library working through the results section, surrounded by datasets and statistical output. I put my head down and worked through mine in a few hours.

I later discovered some of my classmates had spent weeks on it.

That was probably one of the first signs that analytical work came quite naturally to me. More importantly, I realised I actually enjoyed doing it.

### From Sport to Data

During university, a lecture from a visiting Sports Data Analyst opened my eyes to the possibility of building a career around data in sport.

That led to a placement with **Nova Centurion Swimming Club**, where I gained my first experience working with data in an elite sporting environment. I worked with large datasets, from collecting and cleaning data through to analysing performance and turning the results into actionable insights. More importantly, it showed me how data could be used to understand performance, inform decisions, and drive improvement.

At the same time, I was working at **Halfords**, where I started at 16 building bikes at weekends before progressing into a full-time role during COVID. Over the years, I worked across almost every aspect of the business, from bike builds and workshop repairs to customer service, stock management and sales, eventually progressing to **Department Manager**.

As my responsibilities grew, so did my understanding of the commercial side of the business. I gained first-hand experience of how inventory, pricing, promotions, customer demand, staffing and operational decisions influence performance, while balancing commercial targets with the realities of running a department day to day.

By the time I finished university, I had experience on two quite different sides of a business: working with data to understand performance, and working within a business to deliver it.

### Realising I liked the analysis more than the subject

After university, I started looking at roles in sports data. I was still interested in working in sport, but I was also beginning to question whether the industry itself was what I wanted to build my career around. I had a good job as a Department Manager, but I didn't see myself doing it long-term, so I started thinking more carefully about what I actually enjoyed about the work I had done so far.

Sports data was a relatively niche career path, and there weren't many entry-level opportunities. Rather than seeing that as a reason to give up on the idea, it made me think more broadly about where the skills I had developed could take me.

The more I thought about it, the clearer it became that the part I enjoyed most wasn't necessarily **sport**.

It was **analysis**.

I liked taking information, finding patterns, understanding what was happening, and using that understanding to make something better. Once I recognised that, the possibilities became much wider.

Not long after, I interviewed at **SGS Engineering**. The combination of data analysis and several years of commercial retail experience turned out to be surprisingly relevant. I joined SGS and found myself working at the intersection of data, ecommerce, products, and commercial operations.

And that's where I am now.

# Why I Built This Site


This website started as a much smaller project.

Originally, it was little more than a home for some of the sports data analysis work I completed at university. At the time, the goal wasn't to create a professional portfolio. I simply wanted somewhere to publish projects, experiment with web technologies, and learn a little more about how websites worked behind the scenes.

As my career developed, so did the purpose of the site.

What began with the intention to host academic work, has gradually evolved into a portfolio, blog, and personal knowledge base. Today, it serves as a record of the projects I'm working on, the technologies I'm exploring, and the lessons I'm picking up along the way. Some posts are technical, some are professional, and some are simply things I've found interesting enough to document.

In many ways, the site acts as a public notebook. It's where I document projects, ideas, and things I'm learning, often in more detail than would ever fit on a CV or social media post.

That idea of ownership is important to me. Rather than relying solely on platforms where content quickly disappears into a feed, I wanted a space that was entirely my own. The content is mine, the structure is mine, and the direction of the site is mine.

This also influenced the technical choices behind the site.

It's built using Hugo, a static site generator that keeps things intentionally simple. Every page and post exists as a Markdown file, which means the content itself remains lightweight, portable, and easy to maintain. There are no databases to manage and no complex backend to worry about. I can focus on writing rather than maintaining infrastructure.

The site is hosted through GitHub, making it completely free to run while also providing version control for every change. More importantly, everything remains recoverable. If the website were to disappear tomorrow, the content would still exist as a collection of Markdown files stored locally and backed up through Git and Syncthing. The website is simply one way of presenting that information.

I like that separation. The content belongs to me, while the website is just a layer on top.

Of course, there are always ideas for improvements. New designs, features, layouts, and experiments inevitably find their way onto a growing list of things I'd like to try. For now, though, I'm far more interested in building the content than endlessly refining the presentation.

A website can be beautifully designed, but if there's nothing meaningful behind it, the polish doesn't count for much. I'd rather spend my time documenting projects and sharing what I'm learning.

And so, despite becoming a portfolio, a blog, and a professional resource, this website remains what it has always been: a personal project.
