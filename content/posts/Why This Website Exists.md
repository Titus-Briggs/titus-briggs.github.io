This website started as a much smaller project.

Originally, it was little more than a home for some of the sports data analysis work I completed at university. At the time, the goal wasn't to create a professional portfolio. I simply wanted somewhere to publish projects, experiment with web technologies, and learn a little more about how websites worked behind the scenes.

As my career developed, so did the purpose of the site.

What began with the intention to host academic work, has gradually evolved into a portfolio, blog, and personal knowledge base. Today, it serves as a record of the projects I'm working on, the technologies I'm exploring, and the lessons I'm picking up along the way. Some posts are technical, some are professional, and some are simply things I've found interesting enough to document.

In many ways, the site acts as a public notebook. It's where I document projects, ideas, and things I'm learning, often in more detail than would ever fit on a CV or social media post.

That idea of ownership is important to me. Rather than relying solely on platforms where content quickly disappears into a feed, I wanted a space that was entirely my own. The content is mine, the structure is mine, and the direction of the site is mine.

This also influenced the technical choices behind the site.

It's built using Hugo, a static site generator that keeps things intentionally simple. Every page and post exists as a Markdown file, which means the content itself remains lightweight, portable, and easy to maintain. There are no databases to manage and no complex backend to worry about. I can focus on writing rather than maintaining infrastructure.

The site is hosted through GitHub, making it completely free to run while also providing version control for every change. More importantly, everything remains recoverable. If the website were to disappear tomorrow, the content would still exist as a collection of Markdown files stored locally and backed up through Git. The website is simply one way of presenting that information.

I like that separation. The content belongs to me, while the website is just a layer on top.

Of course, there are always ideas for improvements. New designs, features, layouts, and experiments inevitably find their way onto a growing list of things I'd like to try. For now, though, I'm far more interested in building the content than endlessly refining the presentation.

A website can be beautifully designed, but if there's nothing meaningful behind it, the polish doesn't count for much. I'd rather spend my time documenting projects and sharing what I'm learning.

And so, despite becoming a portfolio, a blog, and a professional resource, this website remains what it has always been: a personal project.
